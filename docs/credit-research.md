# Credit Dragon research notes

## Opening statistic

“In ASIC’s 2021–22 survey, 19% of buy now, pay later users surveyed reported at least two signs of financial stress.”

The optional comparison is 8% of credit card holders in the same survey. ASIC defines financial stress here as two or more indicators, which include difficulty paying utilities or rent, going without meals, and seeking help. Survey fieldwork ran from April 2021 to March 2022. The comparison describes survey responses and cannot establish what caused the financial stress. [ASIC submission to Treasury, pp. 14–15, Figure 5](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-02/c2022-338372-asic.pdf#page=15)

A backup statistic for a bill-focused hook is that about one in five BNPL consumers ASIC surveyed reported missing or paying other bills late so they could make their BNPL payments on time during the preceding 12 months. This is from a separate 2020 report. [ASIC Report 672, p. 16](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/5852803/rep672-published-16-november-2020-2.pdf#page=16)

## What we can say about credit

Equifax Australia calculates its consumer credit score using information from a credit report. It lists credit enquiries and applications, repayment history, adverse events, and credit account information among the factors. Some credit providers do not supply repayment history to Equifax, so the team should avoid assuming how any BNPL provider reports or whether a specific transaction appears in a credit report. [Equifax score explainer](https://equifax.com.au/personal/node/252) · [Equifax credit reports help](https://www.equifax.com.au/personal/help-centre/credit-reports)

## Demo wording

“A cheaper phone plan can leave Mia more room in her budget for upcoming repayments. Credit Dragon helps her track the habits she’s working on.”

Label the on-screen metric “Goal progress” or “Illustrative demo score.” Treat Mia’s late repayment and bounced gym debit as prompts for a conversation about payment timing. The prototype’s transaction data cannot establish whether either event appeared in a credit report or changed a bureau score.
